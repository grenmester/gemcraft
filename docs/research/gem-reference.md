# Gem Reference for *Gemstone Collector*

> **Purpose:** a researched reference for choosing the first-iteration Gemdex roster and grounding the game's systems in real gemology.
> **Date:** 2026-07-25
> **Accuracy note:** Hardness (Mohs), specific gravity (SG), refractive index (RI), crystal system, cleavage, and colors are standard gemological reference values (small ranges reflect natural variation and, for RI, birefringence). "Classic sources" are the historically famous localities most useful for flavor and location design, not current production rankings.

## How this maps to the game

| Real property | Game system it feeds |
| --- | --- |
| Mohs hardness, SG, RI, crystal habit, luster, streak, fluorescence, cleavage | **Identify** — the diagnostic tests and the candidate-narrowing puzzle |
| Deposit type & geological environment | **Explore** — which localities exist and what their find pools are |
| Mining method & difficulty | **Explore** — expedition minigame + gating/difficulty tier of a locality |
| Cleavage, phenomena, treatments | **Cut** — cut choice, shatter risk, phenomenon reveal |
| Rarity & base value | **Economy** — sale price and value progression |
| Natural colors & optical character | **Art direction** — gem rendering/palette |
| Mineral family/group | **Catalog** — Gemdex grouping and family-familiarity |

## 3. Detailed profiles by family/group

Grouped the way the Gemdex should group them (families → familiarity bonuses). Each note leads with **ID keys** (what separates it in the Identify puzzle), then where it forms (→ location), art color, and cut/phenomena/treatment notes.

### Corundum (ruby & sapphire) — Al₂O₃
The same mineral; **ruby** is red (Cr), **sapphire** is everything else (blue from Fe+Ti; also colorless, yellow, pink, and the pink-orange **padparadscha**).
- **ID keys:** Mohs **9** (only diamond is harder), SG ~**4.0** (noticeably heavy), RI 1.76; ruby fluoresces **strong red** under UV. No cleavage (has parting).
- **Forms in:** metamorphic marble (Mogok), alluvial gravels (Sri Lanka, Montana), and basalt. → alluvial *and* metamorphic locations; a great "found in a creek" beginner gem (Montana sapphire) that also appears in high-tier marble sites.
- **Art:** deep red; cornflower/royal blue; the whole pastel range for fancy sapphire.
- **Cut/phenomena:** cabochon reveals **six-rayed stars** (rutile-needle asterism). Almost all commercial ruby/sapphire is **heat-treated**; "unheated" carries a premium. Tough and hard → forgiving to cut.
- **Design hook:** the historical **ruby ↔ red spinel** confusion (the crown-jewel "Black Prince's Ruby" is spinel) is a perfect Identify puzzle (separate by SG/RI/fluorescence).

### Beryl — Be₃Al₂Si₆O₁₈
One mineral, many gems by trace element: **emerald** (green, Cr/V), **aquamarine** (blue, Fe), **morganite** (pink, Mn), **heliodor** (yellow), **red beryl** (red, Mn — one of Earth's rarest gems, effectively only Utah).
- **ID keys:** Mohs 7.5–8, SG ~**2.7** (light for its size), RI ~1.57. Emerald is typically **heavily included** ("jardin"); aquamarine is typically very clean.
- **Forms in:** **pegmatites** (aquamarine, morganite, heliodor) and **hydrothermal schist veins** (Colombian emerald). → pegmatite locations for the blues/pinks; a special metamorphic/vein location for emerald.
- **Art:** vivid green; sky/sea blue; peach-pink; golden yellow.
- **Cut/phenomena:** the **emerald cut** (step cut) exists partly to protect this brittle, included stone; emeralds are routinely **oiled/resin-filled**.

### Quartz — SiO₂ (the workhorse family)
Macrocrystalline: **clear/rock crystal, amethyst** (purple), **citrine** (yellow), **rose** (pink), **smoky** (brown), **ametrine** (bi-color). Microcrystalline (chalcedony): **agate** (banded), **carnelian, onyx, jasper, tiger's-eye, chrysoprase**.
- **ID keys:** Mohs **7**, SG **2.65**, RI 1.55, **no cleavage / conchoidal fracture**, inert under UV. These constants make quartz the "reference" mid-hardness gem.
- **Forms in:** everywhere — pegmatites, hydrothermal veins, **geodes/vugs** (amethyst), sedimentary nodules (agate). → the natural filler across most locations; **amethyst geodes** anchor a hydrothermal/geode location.
- **Art:** the full spectrum but usually softer/paler than the "precious" gems — good for a broad, friendly starter palette.
- **Design hook:** the classic **amethyst ↔ purple fluorite** puzzle (7 vs 4 Mohs, no cleavage vs perfect cleavage, inert vs strongly fluorescent). Heat turns amethyst into most commercial "citrine."

### Garnet group — X₃Y₂(SiO₄)₃ (a whole family of species)
Not one mineral but a group sharing cubic structure and dodecahedral habit: **almandine** (red, common), **pyrope** (red), **rhodolite** (purplish-red, pyrope-almandine), **spessartine** (orange), **grossular** (→ **tsavorite** green, **hessonite** orange), **andradite** (→ **demantoid** green, high fire).
- **ID keys:** all **cubic, no cleavage**, dodecahedral crystals; but SG and RI **spread widely by species** (SG 3.6 grossular → 4.2 spessartine; RI 1.74 → 1.89 demantoid). So the group is a built-in Identify sub-puzzle: color narrows, SG/RI separates.
- **Forms in:** metamorphic schist/gneiss (almandine, tsavorite), pegmatite (spessartine), alluvial gravels, even kimberlite (pyrope — a **diamond indicator**). → garnets belong in many locations; use the rare greens (tsavorite/demantoid) as high-tier metamorphic finds.
- **Art:** the reds, a vivid mandarin orange, and two distinct greens.
- **Design hook:** completing the garnet family is a satisfying multi-species Gemdex set; pyrope-as-diamond-indicator is a real prospecting tell for a diamond location.

### Feldspar group — the phenomenal stones
**Moonstone** (orthoclase, blue/white **adularescence**), **labradorite/spectrolite** (**labradorescence** — spectral flash), **amazonite** (green microcline), **sunstone** (**aventurescence** — copper shimmer).
- **ID keys:** Mohs 6–6.5, SG ~2.6, **two cleavage directions ~90°**, RI ~1.53. The *optical effect* is the giveaway more than the numbers.
- **Forms in:** pegmatites, and labradorite in igneous gabbro/anorthosite. → surface/pegmatite locations.
- **Art:** these are all about the **sheen/schiller** — great candidates to show off phenomena in the Cut phase (cabochon reveals the effect).

### Chrysoberyl — BeAl₂O₄
Three faces: ordinary **chrysoberyl** (yellow-green), **cymophane** (cat's-eye — sharp chatoyant line), and **alexandrite** (green in daylight, red under incandescent — **color change**).
- **ID keys:** Mohs **8.5** (very hard), SG 3.7, RI 1.75. Alexandrite's color change and cat's-eye's sharp eye are diagnostic on their own.
- **Forms in:** pegmatites and metamorphic; alluvial. → mid/high-tier locations. Alexandrite is Epic–Legendary.

### Standalone silicates & others
- **Topaz** (Al₂SiO₄(F,OH)₂): Mohs **8**, SG 3.53, **perfect basal cleavage** (must be oriented carefully → cut risk), pegmatite. Colorless, blue (usually irradiated), imperial pink-orange, sherry. → pegmatite location.
- **Spinel** (MgAl₂O₄): Mohs 8, SG 3.6, **cubic octahedra, no cleavage**, red/pink/blue/lavender. Marble & alluvial (Mogok, Sri Lanka). The great historical impostor for ruby → prime Identify puzzle. Underrated, rising value.
- **Zircon** (ZrSiO₄): Mohs 6.5–7.5 but **SG 4.6–4.7 (very heavy)** and **RI ~1.95 with strong doubling** — those two make it easy to ID and a good "heft/loupe" teaching stone. Alluvial. Blue zircon is heated. (Not cubic zirconia — that's synthetic.)
- **Peridot** (olivine): Mohs 6.5–7, SG 3.34, RI 1.65–1.69 with visible **doubling**, olive-green only. Basalt & mantle xenoliths; also **found in meteorites** (pallasites). → volcanic/surface location; a fun "space rock" flavor hook.
- **Tanzanite** (zoisite): Mohs 6.5–7, SG 3.35, **one perfect cleavage**, **strong trichroism** (blue/violet/burgundy). **Only from Tanzania.** Almost always **heat-treated** (brown → blue). → single high-tier metamorphic location; teaches pleochroism (→ cut orientation).
- **Iolite** (cordierite): Mohs 7–7.5, SG **2.6 (light)**, dramatic pleochroism (blue/gray/yellow) — "Viking's compass." Cheap, underused, great pleochroism teacher.
- **Kunzite/Hiddenite** (spodumene): Mohs 6.5–7, **two perfect cleavages** (notoriously hard to cut), pink/green, pegmatite, color can fade. → pegmatite; a cut-risk teaching stone.
- **Jade**: two different minerals — **jadeite** (pyroxene, Mohs 7, SG 3.33, imperial green, Myanmar) and **nephrite** (amphibole, Mohs 6–6.5, SG 2.95, the toughest gem material). Metamorphic; often found as **boulders**. Carved, not faceted.
- **Sphene/titanite** (CaTiSiO₅): Mohs 5–5.5 (soft), but **RI 1.84–2.03 and dispersion beating diamond** — spectacular fire. Metamorphic; collector stone.
- **Apatite** (Ca₅(PO₄)₃(F,Cl,OH)): Mohs **5** — the "5" on the Mohs scale itself; neon blue/green; too soft for daily wear → a teaching stone for hardness limits.

### Amorphous & aggregate
- **Opal** (SiO₂·nH₂O): Mohs 5.5–6.5, **SG ~2.1 (light)**, **RI ~1.45 (low)**, amorphous. **Play-of-color** in precious opal is diffraction from silica spheres. Sedimentary (Australia) & volcanic (Ethiopia/Mexico). Soft, water-sensitive; the ultimate phenomenon showcase.
- **Obsidian**: volcanic glass, Mohs 5–5.5, amorphous, conchoidal, black (snowflake, mahogany, rainbow sheen). Surface at lava flows. A great cheap beginner find.
- **Turquoise / Lapis lazuli**: opaque blues. Turquoise = hydrated Cu-Al phosphate from arid copper weathering; **lapis** = a *rock* (lazurite + pyrite + calcite) from contact metamorphism (Afghanistan). Both carved/cabbed. Lapis's gold pyrite flecks and light-blue streak are diagnostic.
- **Malachite / Azurite** (copper carbonates, Mohs 3.5–4): banded green / deep blue from oxidized copper zones. **Colored streaks** (green/blue) make them the poster children for the streak test. Soft, carved.

### Organics (no crystal structure — "gems" by tradition)
- **Pearl** (aragonite + conchiolin, Mohs ~3): **orient** iridescence; from mollusks (natural rare; cultured common). No cutting — a different collection/appraisal flow.
- **Amber** (fossil tree resin, Mohs 2–2.5): **floats in saltwater** (SG ~1.05 — a unique, fun diagnostic), warm colors, **fluoresces blue-green**, can contain trapped insects. Baltic/Dominican, from sediments and beaches.

| **Pegmatite** | Coarse, volatile-rich final melt of granite → big crystals in pockets | **Hard-rock / pocket** work | 3 | tourmaline, aquamarine & beryls, topaz, spodumene (kunzite), spessartine, morganite | Minas Gerais (Brazil); Pala (California); Maine; Afghanistan |

| **Metamorphic** (marble, schist, gneiss) | Heat & pressure recrystallize rock | **Hard-rock** | 4 | ruby, sapphire, spinel, jade, lapis, tsavorite, tanzanite, garnet | Mogok (Myanmar); Merelani (Tanzania); Afghanistan |
| **Igneous / volcanic** (basalt, rhyolite) | Crystallize in or erupt with lava; mantle xenoliths | **Surface collecting** | 1–2 | peridot, sapphire (basaltic), zircon, obsidian, labradorite, sunstone, red beryl | Arizona (peridot); Oregon (sunstone); Utah (red beryl) |
| **Sedimentary / weathering** | Precipitate in sedimentary rock or form by surface weathering | **Surface / shallow dig** | 2 | opal (Australia), turquoise, malachite, azurite | Coober Pedy (Australia); SW USA (turquoise) |
| **Kimberlite pipe** | Deep magma carries diamonds up from the mantle | **Bulk / deep** | 5 | diamond (with pyrope/chrome-diopside indicators) | Kimberley (S. Africa); Argyle (Australia) |
| **Organic** | Biological (mollusks, fossil resin) | **Diving / beach-combing** | 1–4 | pearl, amber, coral, jet | Persian Gulf, Baltic coast, Dominican Republic |

**Difficulty ramp for the map (suggested):**
`Local creek (panning) → surface/volcanic collecting → geode fields → pegmatite pockets → metamorphic hard-rock → kimberlite / single-source rarities`

## 5. Look-alike puzzles (for Identify)

Deliberately seed these real, historically meaningful confusions so identification stays a genuine deduction:

| Look-alike set | What they share | What separates them (the diagnostic test) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diamond / white sapphire / zircon / quartz / topaz (colorless) | Colorless, glassy | Hardness (7→10) + **SG** (2.65 → 4.7) + RI + zircon's doubling |
| **Ruby / red spinel / pyrope garnet** (the historic one) | Red | SG (4.0 vs 3.6 vs 3.7), fluorescence, spinel's octahedral habit, garnet no fluor |

| Emerald / green tourmaline / tsavorite / peridot | Green | RI/SG, inclusions, peridot's doubling, garnet's isotropy |
| Blue sapphire / tanzanite / iolite | Blue | Hardness (9 vs 6.5 vs 7), tanzanite & iolite's strong **pleochroism**, SG |

## 6. Phenomena (for the Cut phase's "wow" moments)

| Phenomenon | Cause | Gems | Revealed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Asterism** (star) | Oriented needle inclusions (rutile) | star sapphire/ruby, star garnet, star rose quartz | Cabochon, oriented |
| **Chatoyancy** (cat's-eye) | Parallel fibers/tubes | chrysoberyl (cymophane), tiger's-eye, some tourmaline | Cabochon, oriented |
| **Play-of-color** | Diffraction from silica spheres | precious opal | Cabochon / dome |
| **Labradorescence** | Light interference in lamellae | labradorite/spectrolite | Flat/cab to catch the flash |
| **Adularescence** | Scattering in feldspar layers | moonstone | Cabochon |
| **Aventurescence** | Reflective platelet inclusions | sunstone, aventurine | Cabochon |
| **Color change** | Balanced absorption, light-dependent | alexandrite, some garnet/sapphire | Any; needs two light sources to see |
| **Iridescence / orient** | Thin-film / surface structure | pearl, ammolite, fire agate | Natural surface |
| **Dispersion** ("fire") | Splitting of white light | diamond, demantoid, sphene, zircon | Brilliant/faceted cuts |
| **Pleochroism** | Different color per crystal axis | tanzanite, iolite, ruby, andalusite | Orientation choice when cutting |

## 7. Art-direction notes (natural colors)

- **Single-color anchors** (unambiguous palette): peridot = olive-green, malachite = banded green, turquoise = robin's-egg blue, lapis = deep blue + gold flecks, rhodochrosite = pink banding.
- **Multi-color families** (one shape, a spectrum of hues — efficient art reuse): quartz, beryl, tourmaline, garnet, sapphire, spinel, topaz, fluorite.
- **"Effect" gems** (the art is the *motion/sheen*, not a flat color): opal (play-of-color), labradorite (flash), moonstone (adularescent glow), star sapphire (star), alexandrite (color change). These need special shader/animation treatment and are the visual showpieces.
- **Luster ladder** for material shading: adamantine (diamond, demantoid, sphene, zircon) → vitreous (most) → waxy (turquoise, chalcedony) → greasy (jade) → resinous (amber) → pearly (moonstone, pearl) → silky/dull (malachite).
- **Crystal habit for the "rough" art:** hexagonal prisms (quartz, beryl, corundum), cubes/octahedra (fluorite, diamond, spinel, pyrite), dodecahedra (garnet), bladed/prismatic (kyanite, tourmaline), botryoidal blobs (malachite, hematite), massive/banded (agate, jade, lapis).

## 8. Recommended first-iteration roster

Goal: a compact set that (a) covers several **families** (so family-completion/familiarity is meaningful), (b) spans the **deposit types & difficulty tiers** (so the map has variety), (c) seeds good **look-alike puzzles**, (d) includes at least one of each **phenomenon type** you want to show, and (e) gives a satisfying **rarity/value curve**. The current build already has 8 of these.

**Tier 1 — starter (alluvial creek + easy surface), Common/Uncommon:**
`clear quartz, amethyst, citrine, agate` (quartz family — a completable set), `almandine garnet`, `obsidian`, `peridot` *(new)*

**Tier 2 — geode & pegmatite, Uncommon/Rare:**
`fluorite` (soft + cleavage + UV teaching stone), `topaz` (cleavage/cut risk), `aquamarine` *(new — beryl family)*, `tourmaline` *(new — pleochroism)*, `moonstone` *(new — adularescence phenomenon)*

**Tier 3 — metamorphic hard-rock, Rare/Epic:**
`sapphire`, `ruby` *(new)*, `spinel` *(new — the ruby look-alike)*, `emerald` *(new — beryl family, oiled/included)*, `tsavorite` *(new — completes a garnet mini-set with almandine)*

**Tier 4 — trophy / single-source & kimberlite, Epic/Legendary:**
`tanzanite` *(new — pleochroism + single source)*, `opal` *(new — play-of-color showpiece)*, `alexandrite` *(new — color change)*, `diamond` *(new — the kimberlite capstone)*

**Why this set works**
- **Families with real sets to complete:** quartz (4), beryl (aquamarine + emerald, room to grow), garnet (almandine + tsavorite), corundum (ruby + sapphire) — familiarity bonuses become meaningful, not single-member trivia.
- **Every deposit type is represented**, so all Explore methods (pan / surface / geode / pegmatite / hard-rock / kimberlite) have a reason to exist.
- **Look-alike puzzles baked in:** ruby↔spinel, amethyst↔fluorite, colorless quartz↔topaz↔sapphire↔diamond, sapphire↔tanzanite, emerald↔tsavorite.
- **One of each phenomenon:** asterism (sapphire), adularescence (moonstone), play-of-color (opal), color-change (alexandrite), plus pleochroism (tanzanite/tourmaline) and dispersion (diamond) for the Cut phase.
- **Clean value curve:** quartz/obsidian (single digits) → garnet/peridot/topaz (tens) → tourmaline/spinel/aquamarine (hundreds) → ruby/sapphire/emerald/tanzanite → alexandrite/diamond (thousands+).

**Growth path (later iterations):** widen each family (add morganite/heliodor to beryl; spessartine/demantoid to garnet; the other quartz & feldspar varieties), add the organics (amber's float test, pearl's different flow), and the collector rarities (sphene's fire, red beryl, padparadscha) as trophy content.
